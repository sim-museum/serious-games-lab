SESSION ANALYSIS — PokerIQ Local, $1/$2 NL 6-max
================================================

SESSION SUMMARY
---------------
A very short three-hand sample. You folded the first two hands and won a large pot in the third, finishing +$82 (from $200 to $282). You were never at showdown, so almost all the information here is pre-flop discipline plus one well-played post-flop confrontation in Hand #3. The data set is too small to draw bankroll conclusions, but it does show a clean, position-aware folding pattern and one aggressive line that ran well — though for reasons worth examining honestly.

KEY HANDS
---------

HAND #1 — Button, Q8o, folded to limps
What happened: You were on the button (dealer) with Q8o. Two players limped/checked ahead and you folded.
Assessment: The fold is defensible, but the button over a limped pot is exactly where you can apply pressure. Q8o is a weak holding, but in position against two passive limpers (Loose Bruce and a checked big blind), a small "isolation/limp-raise" to ~$8–10 is a standard exploitative play — you take the pot down often and realize equity in position when called. Folding here is the low-variance choice and not a mistake, but recognize you are leaving "dead money" on the table.
Concept: Small Stakes Hold'em (Miller/Sklansky/Malmuth) — punishing limpers from late position; and Sklansky's idea of betting when fold equity plus pot equity exceeds your cost.

HAND #2 — UTG-ish, 97o, folded
What happened: 97o, folded pre-flop.
Assessment: Correct and easy. 97o out of position with no pot-sweetening incentive is a clear muck. No notes needed — this is the discipline you want as a default.
Concept: Harrington on Hold'em — early-position hand selection; tight is right when you'll be out of position post-flop.

HAND #3 — K8o, 3-bet pre, flopped top pair, won $82
What happened: Aggro Angela raised to $6, you 3-bet to $12 with K8o, she called. Flop Jd Ks 6c gave you top pair (K). She min-raised, you re-raised, she 3-bet, you jammed to $130, she folded. You won $82.
Assessment — the result was great, but separate the result from the decision:
- Pre-flop: 3-betting K8o is the loose part of this hand. K8o is dominated by every Kx that calls (KT, KJ, KQ, AK) and flips at best against her actual range. Against a known aggressive, wide opener (her range here is genuinely junky), a light 3-bet has merit as an exploit — but K8o specifically is a hand that plays poorly when called because of its kicker problems. A hand like a suited connector or a Kx-suited would 3-bet better.
- Post-flop: Once you flop top pair on Jd Ks 6c with 95% equity against her 3h Th (basically air with a backdoor flush draw), the aggression is correct and you maximized value. Re-raising her flop min-raise and getting stacks in is exactly right — you have a huge edge and want money in while she's "spewing."
- The tell: Her min-raise on the flop ($13) is a classic weak/probe sizing. Against a strong made hand you can comfortably escalate; she revealed a capped, weak range and you punished it.
Concept: Sklansky's Fundamental Theorem of Poker — you profited every time she put money in drawing nearly dead. Also Janda (Applications of NLHE) on capitalizing when villain's range is uncapped-weak. But note Professional No-Limit Hold'em's warning: don't let a winning result validate a thin pre-flop 3-betting standard.

ON THE BOT FIELD (context, not your decisions)
The "RANGE ACCURACY" readout shows the opponents are playing almost entirely out of any sensible range (J2o, T7o, 76o opening). Practically, this means your edge here is to value-bet relentlessly and bluff rarely — against players who call/raise with junk, thin value beats fancy bluffs. This is the core lesson of Playing the Player (Miller): adapt to the population, don't play a GTO baseline against people who can't fold.

IMPROVEMENT SUGGESTIONS
-----------------------
1. Exploit limpers from the button. In spots like Hand #1, default to a raise with playable-ish hands rather than a check/fold. Position + dead money is your highest-EV recurring edge against passive fields.

2. Tighten your light 3-betting hand selection. If you're going to 3-bet pre-flop against loose openers (a good instinct vs Aggro Angela), choose hands that flop better when called — suited Kx, suited connectors, suited aces — rather than offsuit, kicker-dominated hands like K8o. The result won't always bail you out.

3. Value-bet wider, bluff less against this field. With opponents calling with bottom pair and high cards (Hands #1 and #2 went to showdown with very weak holdings), get more thin value and avoid elaborate bluffs that have no fold equity.

4. Read sizing tells. Angela's flop min-raise in Hand #3 signaled weakness — bank that pattern. Small, probing raises from aggressive players are usually merge/weak; escalate with your strong hands and don't give them a cheap card.

5. Gather a bigger sample before drawing conclusions. Three hands and zero showdowns isn't enough to assess leaks or bankroll trajectory. Aim for a few hundred hands so the math (win rate, showdown winnings, 3-bet success) becomes meaningful — Mathematics of Poker reminds us short-run results are dominated by variance.

Net: disciplined folds, one well-extracted big pot, and one pre-flop standard (K8o 3-bet) to firm up. Keep the aggression — just point it at slightly better hands.
