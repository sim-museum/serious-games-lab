IRTEST-261015 -- iRacing tests for julia racer PHYSFID-1 (about 30 minutes)
Car: Lotus 49. Record telemetry (Alt+L) for every test. Wheel settings as usual (they are read from the .ibt).

1. Skidpad limit, steady state, both setups (about 10 min)
   - Skidpad, DEFAULT setup. Drive the circle clockwise at a steady speed, then raise the speed in small steps
     (hold each step about 5 s) until the front starts to run wide. Keep the throttle steady at each step.
   - Repeat anticlockwise.
   - Repeat both with the WW103 (loose) setup.
   Why: tells where iRacing's front tyres give up compared with the rears (julia's front/rear grip split).

2. Straight full-throttle pulls, both setups, same gear (about 10 min)
   - Monza GP without chicanes, main straight, DEFAULT setup: from about 120 km/h in 4th gear, full throttle until the
     braking point. Three pulls.
   - The same with WW103 (its 4th gear has the same 1.04 ratio).
   Why: iRacing's WW103 puts about 6 % more drive down than its default at the same gear ratio and rpm; julia does
   not. Same gear, same road, same weather isolates the setup's effect.

3. Coast in gear, both setups (about 5 min)
   - Same straight: at about 250 km/h lift completely (clutch engaged, no brake) and coast down to 100 km/h in gear.
     Twice per setup.
   Why: separates the drivetrain's losses under power from its losses under engine braking.

PART 2 (added 2026-10-15): how iRacing handles OTHER CARS -- contact and drafting (CARINTERACT-1)
julia today: contact between cars has almost no downside ("bumper cars"); the draft is a fixed rule (up to 45 % less
drag within 26 m behind a car, 2.8 m either side). iRacing is the reference for both.

Setting up an AI race in iRacing (check each step on screen -- the menus change between iRacing builds):
  * Main menu: "Play" (or "Race") -> "AI Racing" -> "Create a Race". The car list there shows only AI-enabled cars:
    look for the Lotus 49. If it is NOT offered, use the closest open-wheeler that is (e.g. a Formula Vee or Skip
    Barber) and say which -- contact and draft behaviour is still iRacing's, the numbers then belong to that car.
  * Track: any AI-enabled config you own -- preferably Monza (a long straight for the draft test) or Watkins Glen.
    The track list in Create a Race is also filtered to AI-enabled configs.
  * SLOWING THE AI: the "Opponents" step has an AI strength / skill range slider -- set it to the LOWEST values so the
    AI lap well below your pace. The AI roster editor (Create a Race -> roster -> edit drivers) also has per-driver
    Skill and Aggression sliders; set 2-3 drivers to minimum skill and low aggression. Ask for 3-5 AI cars.
  * Sessions: add a Practice session (AI run in it too) -- it is the calmest place to catch and touch cars.
  * Damage: leave damage ON (realistic) so contact has its consequences. Record telemetry (Alt+L) throughout.

4. Contact (about 10 min, practice session)
   a. Light nudge: catch a slow AI car on a straight, match its speed, then touch its gearbox with your nose at a
      closing speed of about 5 km/h. Back off. Repeat at about 15 km/h. Note on screen any damage/repair message.
   b. Side-by-side: run alongside an AI car and lean into it gently for 1-2 s (wheel-to-wheel touch).
   c. A harder hit (optional, last): about 30 km/h closing into the back of a slow AI car.
   After each, drive one slow lap: does the car pull to one side, is anything bent, did the incident counter move
   (iRacing shows 0x/1x/2x/4x)? Say what you saw in a sentence per test (voice note or text is fine).
   Why: the .ibt records our car's accelerations at each touch, the incident count (PlayerCarMyIncidentCount) and any
   repair time (PitRepairLeft); with your notes that gives iRacing's contact law (how hard a touch is felt, what it
   costs), which julia then copies -- touching cars should hurt you as it does in iRacing.

5. Drafting and slingshot (about 10 min, Monza's main straight or the back straight)
   a. Alone: two full-throttle runs down the straight from the same corner exit, nobody within 100 m. (Baseline.)
   b. Behind: follow an AI car (slowest skill) out of the same corner and stay directly behind it down the straight,
      gap as small as you dare, two runs. Then pull out to the side about 50 m before the braking point (slingshot).
   c. Gap steps: one run each sitting about 10, 20 and 40 m behind.
   Why: the .ibt holds the gap to the car ahead (CarDistAhead) and our speed/acceleration; the speed gained against
   the lone runs, by gap and by sideways offset, is iRacing's draft -- julia's 26 m / 45 % rule is then fitted to it.

(Online alternative if the Lotus 49 has no AI: a hosted session with one friend in Lotus 49s does both tests the
same way -- the friend drives slowly and holds a straight line.)
